            <h2>Introduction</h2>
            <p>2026-08-14---2026-08-20</p>
            <p>I wanted to spend a few days somewhere cool this summer, and after weighing travel time and cost, Da Lat, Vietnam, seemed like the best fit. At around 1,500 meters above sea level, it stays fairly cool, usually around 18–24°C throughout the day, with plenty of clouds and rain. Because of its French colonial history, the city has a lot of European-style architecture, which gives it a pretty distinctive feel compared with much of Southeast Asia. That said, getting here was probably the most hardcore travel itinerary I’ve ever done: a 1.5-hour drive from Haidian in Beijing to Daxing Airport, a three-hour flight to Guangzhou for a connection, another three-hour flight to Ho Chi Minh City, and finally an eight-hour sleeper bus ride to Da Lat. I’m glad I did this while I’m still young—doing the same trip when I’m older might take half my life out of me.</p>
        </section>

            <h2>1. Immigration</h2>
            <p>2026-08-15 <i class="fas fa-sun"></i></p>
            <p>Of all the places I’ve traveled to, immigration in Ho Chi Minh City was by far the most ridiculous. I landed from Guangzhou at 2:00 a.m. local time on August 15, and it took a full hour and a half just to get through immigration. By the time I finally made it out of the airport, it was almost 4:00 a.m., and I was completely wiped out. To be fair, that ended up being the worst part of the entire Vietnam trip. The staff somehow reminded me a little too much of civil servants back home, haha. Once I got through immigration, everything got much better.</p>

            <h2>2. Sleeper Bus</h2>
            <p>2026-08-15 <i class="fas fa-cloud"></i></p>
            <p>After getting through immigration, I slept for a few hours at a hotel near the Ho Chi Minh City airport, then headed out around noon to catch the bus to Da Lat. Google Maps said the drive would take six or seven hours, but it ended up taking more than eight. With rain along the way and a long uphill climb into the mountains, it definitely wasn’t an easy drive. The sleeper berths were also smaller than I expected. I had seen travel tips saying a couple could just buy one berth and share it, but once we got on board, we realized it was way too cramped. The berths are probably designed with local passengers in mind, and with two foreigners sharing one, there was no way for us to fully stretch out our legs.</p>
